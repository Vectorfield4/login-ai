A corporate redesign usually starts with layouts, and by the second feedback round the argument is about taste. Research before a redesign turns that argument into numbers. It rests on five interviews on one segment, a list of five tasks on the current journey and a baseline of four metrics captured before the first change. The protocol takes two weeks and costs RUB 20,000 to 40,000 on top of design. The base design stage, which covers supplied materials and an expert review of journeys, fits RUB 60,000 to 95,000 for five distinct screens. A CMO compares both figures against the cost per lead from organic traffic, and the measurement shows which changes lower it.

## What the pre-redesign measurement buys

A layout answers what the page looks like. Research answers where a person stops and what they do instead of the intended action. The difference shows up in the estimate. Changing an approved layout opens a new feedback round, while a journey gap found before the layout changes page structure while it is still a prototype.

The protocol produces three artifacts: the list of tasks a person fails, a findings table with frequency and cost, and a baseline of four numbers. All three go into the designer brief and into acceptance. The boundary is set in advance. Five interviews describe the motives inside one segment, not its share of traffic. Shares come from analytics, and the interviews explain why those shares look the way they do.

## What to prepare

- Twelve invitations per segment: five participants, two replacements for no-shows, five screened out.
- The current website plus access to three months of analytics, or one week of manual counting.
- Five tasks a person completes on the site: find a service, compare options, check a price, send an enquiry, find a document.
- A recording and consent for it, 30 minutes per person.
- One person with the authority to approve the stop rule. Usually that is the site owner, and the contractor only applies the rule.

Preparation check: all five items are marked. An empty analytics item does not block the start: the manual count in step 4 does not close it, and two metrics cannot be captured without a counter.

## Step 1. Screener: five questions before the invitation

The screener removes anyone who has not done the task in the last three months. Five questions: role, task frequency, date of the last case, device, and the person who approves the decision. The answer "I looked at it once" sends the candidate to the reserve list, because a memory of a task from three years ago describes a different website.

Per segment, twelve invitations break down into five seats, two replacement invitations for no-shows and five screened out. The client, the sales person and the partner divide those five seats among themselves rather than adding to them. If four people pass out of the twelve invitations, add six invitations: the screener questions do not change. A relaxed screener lets through people who never ran the task, and the conversation then describes a different website.

Step check: a table of five rows where each participant has a role, a task frequency and the date of the last case.

## Step 2. Interview script: 30 minutes, five blocks

The script keeps the conversation on the last real case instead of opinions about design. Five blocks: what the person did before the site, which screen they started on, where they stopped, what they did instead, and who made the decision afterwards.

Write questions about behaviour: "show me where you looked for the price" instead of "is the menu convenient for you". An opinion about the menu is worth zero in the findings table, while the path to the price is worth one specific screen.

Transcribe the recording the same day, while the intonation is still fresh. A quote goes into the work when a second participant repeated it on the same screen. How many quotes a conversation produces depends on the number of screens, and the plan does not budget that number.

Step check: every finding has a quote and a screen. A finding without a screen becomes a topic for a second conversation, not an item in the task list.

## Step 3. Task list on the current journey

Five tasks cover the whole journey: find a service, compare two options, check a price, send an enquiry, find a document or a contact. Each task has three fields: entry point, success criterion and stopping point.

People run the tasks on the current site, without a layout and without hints. The success criterion is written before the session, otherwise the result is fitted to the observation. Steps 3 and 5 are counted on these same five participants; steps 4 and 6 run per segment, as does the stop rule, and the next segment is a separate run. The step check is a task table that records how many of the five participants completed each task. A task all five completed stays as a control, because it shows the session itself is intact.

## Step 4. Baseline: four numbers

The baseline is the same four metrics before and after the change. The first is the share of visits that reach the form, the second the share of opened forms that get submitted. The third is the median time from entry to submission, the fourth the number of calls and emails per hundred visits.

Capture on one source until the window holds 100 form submissions. If a hundred does not arrive within 14 days, stretch the window to 30 days and write both numbers into the table: the volume and the dates. A comparison against another channel or another season shows the difference between channels, not the effect of the redesign.

Without analytics, a manual count produces two things: the result of five tasks across five sessions and the call log from the sales team. The log gives a dated enquiry count and the sessions give the median time to submission. The share of visits reaching the form stays marked "needs measurement", and the share of opened forms that get submitted is added once the counter is installed.

Five sessions show only a coarse shift: on them a difference smaller than 50 percentage points cannot be told apart from counting error. A ten percent shift needs several hundred observations per measurement, so the baseline from analytics catches it, not the sessions.

Step check: a table of four rows where every number has a date and a source. A row without a date does not count.

## Step 5. Findings table: frequency, cost, screen

The table merges interviews and measurement into one list. Columns: finding, screen, frequency (how many of the conversations named it), baseline metric, loss over the measurement window, priority. The cost of a finding is the loss of its metric over the measurement window: the share from the baseline multiplied by the number of enquiries in the same window.

Priority comes from frequency and cost. A finding named by four people that sits on the path to the form goes first. A finding named once and tied to no metric moves to the observation list.

Step check: the top three rows carry a number from the baseline. A finding without a number stays a hypothesis and does not enter the scope of the first round.

## Step 6. Two feedback rounds on the prototype

The task list becomes the checklist for the first prototype round. The design stage includes two consolidated feedback rounds within the agreed scope: the first closes the findings from the table, the second collects comments on the agreed screens.

Collect comments in one place and tie each one to a screen and a state. A new journey after prototype approval is estimated separately, so findings that reached the table early save one round.

Step check: every finding from the top three rows is either closed by a prototype screen or recorded as deferred with a reason.

## Verifying the result

The protocol holds when five conditions are met. Five interviews from one segment are transcribed, five tasks were run on the current site, and the metrics are captured and dated. The top three rows of the findings table carry numbers, and the first prototype round closes those rows. With analytics the protocol converges on four dated metrics. Without analytics it converges on two dated metrics and one "needs measurement" note; the fourth metric is added once the counter is installed. Compare the task list before and after: the same five tasks, the same success criterion. Then compare against the number from step 3: if the same number of people complete the task, the change returns to review. A shift of one person out of five counts as noise, not as a result.

## The stop rule

The rule is fixed before the first conversation, otherwise it gets fitted to the result. Interviews stop at five when the fifth participant names no new motive. If they do, recruit two more: the series closes at seven when the sixth and seventh conversations produced no new motive. Every extra conversation costs a week of schedule.

The redesign stops at the measurement 14 days after launch. Scale up if the share of visits reaching the form grew and the cost per organic lead did not grow. Return to review if that share has not moved within 14 days; the other three metrics are read as explanation, not as a second trigger.

## How this changes the estimate

Research costs RUB 20,000 to 40,000 on top of design and adds two weeks before the first layout. Design of five distinct screens costs RUB 60,000 to 95,000: RUB 20,000 to 35,000 for structure and concept plus RUB 8,000 to 12,000 per screen.

The saving is counted in rounds. A finding closed in the prototype costs one comment. The same finding after build costs a new screen and a second review. On a corporate site that is the difference between editing structure and moving a finished block.

## What to do this week

1. **Input Artifact: a five-question screener.** Five rows with role, task frequency, date of the last case, device and the person who approves the decision.
2. **Baseline Metric: four numbers before the start.** Share of visits to the form, share of submitted forms, median time to submission, enquiries per hundred visits. Protocol: up to 100 form submissions on one source, 30 days maximum.
3. **Checkpoint Deadline: 14 days after launch.** The same four numbers are captured again on the same source and compared against the baseline date.
4. **Scale-or-Stop Condition.** Spread the change to other segments if the share of visits reaching the form grew and the cost per organic lead did not. Return to review if that share has not moved within 14 days; the other three metrics are read as explanation, not as a second trigger.
